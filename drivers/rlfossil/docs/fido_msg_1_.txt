G'day Jason, I think I have worked out the possible problem. (If you haven't
yet seen my local message, this won't make much sense to you).

Your end is dumping data at high speed to VMODEM, and presumably there is some
sort of buffer. The IP datagram is therefore rather large, so it's sent
fragmented. That's fine, except this end gets sick of waiting for data -- as
the fragments are still being received, the top level application has not yet
been given the datagram, and thinks nothing is coming in. FrontDoor seems
rather impatient, and exclaims "Timeout, Retry n" after only 10 seconds or so.

A simple solution would be a higher speed link to get the fragments here
faster. I'm testing this at 2400 and it runs like a dog (when I am able to
transfer, CPS is about 50). When I get a chance I'll have a play with a 14400
link (I only have a TrailBlazer PEP almost exclusively for remote control of my
system -- connects at 18,000 bps to another PEP, but only 2400 to something
else).

The 14400 link should ease, if not (hopefully) solve the problem entirely.

BTW the software I am using is RLFOSSIL.

I hope you don't mind me using your system to test things out . . . :-)

Cheers.

For those unfamiliar with the explanation above, a brief tutorial. When you send
a block of data via the internet, it's known as a "datagram". The links that the
datagram goes through may not be able to pass the entire datagram in one piece, 
so the IP protocol fragments it into smaller pieces. At the remote end, the
datagram is reassembled, and then passed to the application in its entirety as a
single block.

And there lays the potential problem . . . if someone is sending you a large
datagram then your application may not be given any data for a relatively long
period of time. For an application such as FrontDoor which normally expects to
be connected via modem directly to the remote system, it's not unreasonable for 
it to start getting concerned when no data is presented to it for a period of
time (although IMHO it's a wee bit *too* impatient in that regard).

Internet applications will probably be more lenient and expect long delays,
because that's the nature of the net, and IP fragmentation.
